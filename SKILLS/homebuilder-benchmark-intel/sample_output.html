3B · Where the group converges vs. diverges

Converging (sector dynamics dominate): the direction of margins and earnings — all eight builders are absorbing the same incentive-and-lot-cost squeeze, and all are still adding communities. That uniformity says the pressure is macro, not operator-specific.

Diverging (strategy is the story): the level of margin and the orders response. Gross-margin spread is a wide 9.2 points (15.2% LEN → 24.4% PHM), driven mostly by price tier. And builders split cleanly into "buy the order" (DHI +11%, NVR +7%, TOL +7%) vs. "defend the margin" (TMHC −14%, MTH −5%). The outlier on the right tail is Toll Brothers — the only builder to expand margin guidance.

3C · Outlier spotlight — Toll Brothers

TOL is the most differentiated builder this quarter: a 26.2% adjusted gross margin, orders +7%, and a raised full-year delivery and margin outlook while peers trimmed. Two drivers: (1) luxury/move-up buyers are far less rate-sensitive than entry-level, so TOL didn't have to buy volume with incentives; and (2) a deliberate spec discipline (finished specs cut to ~2 per community) that protected price. The read for everyone else is that the pain is concentrated at the affordability-constrained entry level — exactly where most NC volume sits.

Benchmark Read

This quarter a local NC builder should track the consensus, not the outlier: TOL's luxury insulation doesn't transfer to a Triangle/Charlotte entry or first-move-up operator. The relevant signal is the entry-level group's −330 bps median margin hit and the price-vs-pace fork — decide which side of that fork you're on, explicitly.

4B · Sector consensus outlook

The implied full-year direction is flat-to-down volume with margins bottoming in 1H and trying to stabilize in 2H — Lennar explicitly called its 15.2% Q1 the low point; KBH and PHM are leaning on a build-to-order mix shift to lift back-half margin. Most bullish: Toll, betting on rate-insulated luxury demand and community growth. Most cautious: Lennar (production-first, lowest margin) and Meritage (winter storm + rate shock, choosing margin over a 4/month pace).

4C · Pending corporate events

  • Lennar / Millrose: the early-2025 land-banking spin-off keeps making LEN's balance sheet non-comparable to owned-land peers; watch land-spend and optioned-share disclosures.
  • Toll / Buffington Homes: acquisition extends TOL into Northwest Arkansas and is baked into the raised 480–490 year-end community count.
  • PulteGroup buyback: board added $1.5B to the authorization (to ~$2.1B remaining) — a confidence signal amid the margin dip.
  • Build-to-order pivots: KBH (→70% BTO 2H) and PHM (43%→~60% BTO) are both repositioning the spec/BTO mix, which should raise back-half margin but slow near-term conversion.
Benchmark Read

The guidance band implies a local NC builder should plan next year on flat-to-slightly-down volume and gross margins in the high-teens to low-20s for entry/first-move-up — not a snap-back. If you're modeling a 2H margin recovery, tie it to a concrete mix or cost lever (BTO shift, lot-cost relief), because the publics are only guiding stabilization, not expansion.

SECTION 06

NC market & benchmark intelligence

6A · NC / Southeast segment signals

None of the eight broke out a clean Raleigh or Charlotte number this quarter, so the read is through the East/Southeast segments and product tier. DHI (East/Southeast, #1 in Raleigh) is the single most important NC volume read — entry-level orders +11% bought with ~10% incentives. NVR (Ryan Homes, Mid-Atlantic/SouthEast) and Meritage (East) are the closest pure entry-level Carolinas comps: NVR orders +7% at a flat-ish margin (19.6%), MTH orders −5% as it chose margin (17.5%) over a 4/month pace. PulteGroup (Southeast: Pulte/Centex/Del Webb) and TMHC (East) carry the first-move-up and active-adult read for Charlotte and the Triangle; Toll covers the move-up/luxury tier most local entry operators don't compete in.

6B · Demand drivers (fresh NC figures)

  • Migration: NC added ~145,000–150,000 residents in the year to mid-2025 (pop. 11.2M), the 3rd-largest state gain and #1 for domestic in-migration (Census). Charlotte gained ~21,000 — the most of any major U.S. city — and Raleigh crossed 500,000 for the first time.
  • Affordability: NC statewide median ≈$378,000 (Feb '26, +2.5% YoY, Redfin); Charlotte median ≈$444,000 with sub-$300k homes now under 18% of sales — the affordability gap the entry builders are chasing with incentives.
  • Inventory normalizing: NC entered 2026 with ~57,000 listings (+9.3% YoY) and ~5 months of supply — looser than the national existing-home 4.4 months, tighter than the 9.4-month new-home overhang.
  • Permits cooling: Wake County issued 8,113 residential permits in 2025, down from 9,472 in 2024 — a real slowdown in the Triangle's new-supply engine even as long-run need is 125,000–175,000 units over 10–15 years.

6C · Supply & community pipeline

Community-count growth is broad — DHI +11%, MTH +17%, TOL/PHM +9%, KBH +8%, TMHC +4%. That means the publics are still adding selling communities into the Carolinas corridors even as absorption softens, which keeps competitive new-supply pressure rising in the Triangle and Charlotte. Realtor.com's 2026 forecast cooling Charlotte (14th→70th) and Raleigh (72nd→95th) is consistent with that: maturation, more buyer choice, longer days-on-market — not collapse. Wake County's permit pullback is the counterweight keeping it from tipping into oversupply.

The macro picture is a tug-of-war: demand-side red flags (rates backing up on the Iran conflict, new-home supply at 9.4 months, sales −11% YoY, builder traffic stuck at 25) against structural NC tailwinds (top-ranked in-migration, a frozen resale market locking 85% of owners into sub-5% mortgages, so new construction stays a disproportionate share of available supply). NAHB expects rates to stay slightly above 6% through 2026 with sub-6% unlikely before 2027.

What a nimble local builder can do now that the publics can't: the nationals are committed to community-count growth and even-flow production set quarters ago — they can't easily slow starts in a single soft submarket. A local operator can (1) pace starts community-by-community to the actual Triangle/Charlotte absorption instead of a national plan, avoiding the spec overhang the publics are now working off; (2) redeploy incentive dollars into the specific buydown structures that move your price band rather than a blanket 10–14% load; and (3) lock lots opportunistically while the publics pull back land spend (KBH −38%, TMHC's optioned share rising) — counter-cyclical lot control is the local advantage when the giants are de-risking.

Benchmark Read · Overall

The cycle is in a soft, supply-heavy trough with margins bottoming and rates the swing factor — not a downturn, but not a recovery either. The single biggest thing a local NC builder should act on this quarter: pace starts to real submarket absorption and defend gross margin toward the ~18.6% entry-tier ruler, rather than chasing the volume the nationals are buying with incentives into a 9.4-month supply.
