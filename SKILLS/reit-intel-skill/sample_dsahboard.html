00 Executive Summary — the 30-second read
Sector State

All five REITs positive on same-store revenue — first time in roughly two years.

Q1'26 prints: EXR +1.8%, SMA +1.5%, CUBE +0.6%, NSA +0.9%, PSA +0.1%. NOI now divides into rate-led recovery (EXR, SMA) and expense-led recovery (NSA, PSA, SMA). CUBE's NOI dragged to −1.5% by weather expenses (snow removal +120 bps alone).

Biggest Surprise

NSA delivered the sector's best NOI (+2.0%) — as a closing acquisition target.

NSA cut same-store opex 3.9% YoY (best in sector), driving +2.0% NOI on just +0.9% revenue. SMA was the only REIT to raise guidance (NOI mid from −40 bps to −25 bps). All others reaffirmed.

NC Read-Through

Charlotte and Raleigh-Cary above-avg 2025 supply, but no REIT named the Triangle as a problem market.

NC 2025 deliveries: Charlotte 3.5% of inventory, Raleigh-Cary 3.3% — both above 2.3% national. Yardi raised 2026 supply forecast May 20. REITs flagged Atlanta/Austin/Dallas/Miami/Phoenix; Carolinas escaped explicit problem-market callouts.

Where the sector is converging
Revenue. Q1'26 range of 1.7 pts is the second-tightest of the five quarters, well below Q1'25's 6.4-pt range. A single sector dynamic — supply moderation + demand stabilization + rate-floor reached — is doing the work. Portfolio composition matters less than it did 12 months ago.
Where the sector is diverging
Expense. Q1'26 range of 9.7 pts is the widest of any line item in any quarter in the window. Two stories: (1) weather randomness at CUBE/EXR (snow removal at CUBE alone = 120 bps), (2) genuine operating leverage at NSA, PSA, SMA. Expense discipline is now the differentiator.
Outlier Spotlight NSA delivered the sector's best operating quarter in Q1'26 — +2.0% same-store NOI on −3.9% expenses, the best expense print in the sector — driven by (1) lapping 2024 PRO-consolidation costs, (2) marketing spend that drove +11% YoY move-in volume, and (3) sequential improvement in Atlanta and Phoenix. The irony: this is the operating story management promised for two years, finally delivered just as the company is being acquired by PSA. NSA's relevance to ASSI now is primarily as a benchmark for what disciplined cost control can deliver against a Sun Belt-weighted portfolio.

06 NC Operator Intelligence Brief
6A. NC / Southeast market signals
CUBE's CEO: "Our more stable urban markets in the Northeast and Midwest continue to outperform, while our more transient supply-impacted markets across the Sun Belt and the West Coast are beginning to see green shoots in the form of second-derivative improvement." EXR explicitly named Atlanta, Austin, Dallas, Miami, and Phoenix as continuing supply pressure. PSA: "Sun Belt supply pressures persisted" — coastal and Midwest outperformed. NSA: Atlanta and Phoenix the weakest revenue markets.

No REIT specifically named the Triangle, Charlotte, or Greensboro/High Point as either underperformers or outperformers. The Carolinas continue to fly under the REIT problem-radar — a consistent pattern since 2023 and a constructive signal for ASSI's ownership narrative.

ASSI submarket mapping
Johnston County (Clayton, Angier)
Raleigh-Cary MSA. 3.3% 2025 supply growth above national but well below Atlanta-Phoenix pressure zones. Inflow moderating. REIT silence on Triangle-specific stress is the strongest signal — if PSA/EXR were seeing material Triangle softness, they would have mentioned it.
Wake County suburbs (Fuquay-Varina, Knightdale)
Same Raleigh-Cary MSA dynamics. Sub-MSA / exurban location should perform at-or-better than the MSA blended figure — new development is more capital-intensive at the outer ring.
Chatham County (Pittsboro)
Smaller market, less REIT data. Trajectory most tied to Triangle spillover plus the Chatham Park / VinFast development corridor; structurally constructive demand backdrop.
Cabarrus County (Concord)
Charlotte–Concord–Gastonia MSA (3.5% supply growth, highest NC top metro). Remains ASSI's highest market-risk facility for 2026. Charlotte rent decline noted by REITs but not as severe as Atlanta/Phoenix. Watch facility-level competitor count, not just MSA averages.
Triad / High Point / Greensboro
Internally flagged by ASSI as highest oversupply risk. No REIT explicitly corroborated Triad as a problem market in Q1'26. Either ASSI's internal flag is conservative relative to the REITs, or REIT data is too coarse to catch a Triad-specific issue. Recommend a Yardi sub-MSA pull on Greensboro-High Point to validate or relax this flag against ASSI facility-level rate data for the next ownership check-in.

6E. Rate & pricing intelligence
Competition for move-ins remains intense but the rate floor has been found and rate-side momentum is building. EXR delivered +2.5%/sf new-customer rate growth in Q1; CUBE +2.0% March/April; SMA +2% per-unit. PSA still negative (−2.4%) but improving. NSA RevPAR +0.6%.

ASSI rental-season posture:
  • Web/street rates: sticky-to-slightly-up YoY. REIT consensus: the rate-cutting era is over.
  • Achieved move-in rates: hold published rates; let competitive pressure show up through promotions rather than rate cuts.
  • ECRI: match institutional cadence — EXR's published retention data supports rate elasticity at the existing-customer level.
  • Concession philosophy: SMA's gap (−7%/sf achieved vs +2%/unit) is the clearest tell — discount layer is doing the work, not headline rate cuts. ASSI published rates can hold; concession line is where competition shows up.
So What for ASSI (overall) The Q1'26 tape is the best moment in two-plus years to tell the ASSI ownership story as: (1) the sector has clearly inflected positive — every REIT now positive on revenue, four of five positive on NOI; (2) ASSI's NC submarkets are not in the REIT problem-radar; (3) expense control is the next differentiator and ASSI's tech stack supports that thesis; (4) 2026 budget anchor: PSA caution (−1.1% SS rev) calibrated upward toward EXR neutrality (+0.5%) given ASSI's Triangle exposure.
