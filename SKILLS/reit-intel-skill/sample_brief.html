00 Executive Summary

The 30-second read

  • Sector state

    Positive same-store revenue across all five REITs, but the recovery has split into "rate-led" vs. "expense-led" stories. Q1 2026 is the first quarter all five REITs simultaneously posted positive same-store revenue growth — EXR +1.8%, SMA +1.5%, CUBE +0.6%, NSA +0.9%, PSA +0.1%. The trajectory continues to inflect positive, but expense growth blew out at CUBE (+5.8%) and EXR (+2.7%) due to weather, dragging CUBE's NOI to −1.5%. Conversely, NSA (−3.9% expenses, +2.0% NOI) and SMA (+0.6% expenses, +2.0% NOI) delivered the only positive same-store NOI prints in the sector. The cycle low for revenue is clearly behind us; the question for 2026 is whether expense control can keep up.

  • Biggest surprise

    NSA delivered the highest same-store NOI growth in the sector (+2.0%) despite being a pending merger target. NSA dropped same-store operating expenses 3.9% YoY in Q1 — by far the best expense print in the sector — driven by lower personnel, utilities, and repair costs. This is the operating story management has been promising for two years finally showing up in the numbers, just as the company is being acquired. For ASSI, the read-through is that a Sun Belt–tilted portfolio can deliver positive NOI without rate help if expense discipline is real. SMA also raised guidance — the only REIT to do so — NOI midpoint moved from −40 bps to −25 bps.

  • NC read-through

    Supply is moderating nationally, but Charlotte (+3.5%) and Raleigh-Cary (+3.3%) delivered above-average 2025 inventory growth, and Yardi's Q2 2026 supply forecast was revised up again on May 20. PSA called out "supply-impacted Sun Belt markets" as continued underperformers. EXR explicitly named Atlanta, Austin, Dallas, Miami, and Phoenix — Charlotte was not on the list, but its 3.5% supply addition rate is closer to Atlanta-Phoenix than to the under-supplied Midwest markets the REITs are praising. ASSI's Triangle properties are in the strongest top-25 market for any REIT this cycle; ASSI's Cabarrus/Concord property remains the highest market-risk single facility in the portfolio.

3B. Dispersion commentary

Where the sector is converging
Revenue. Q1'26 high-low range on same-store revenue (1.7 pts: EXR +1.8% to PSA +0.1%) is the second-tightest of the five quarters, well below Q1'25's 6.4-point range. Convergence here means a single sector dynamic — supply moderation plus demand stabilization plus rate-floor reached — is doing most of the work. Individual portfolio composition matters less than it did in 2024.
Where the sector is diverging
Expense. Q1'26 expense range is the widest of any line item or any quarter in the five-quarter window (9.7 pts: NSA −3.9% to CUBE +5.8%). Two stories: (1) weather randomness on the high end (CUBE/EXR snow); (2) genuine operating leverage on the low end (NSA, PSA cost discipline, SMA scale). Expense control is now the most differentiated source of operating outperformance.

3C. Outlier spotlight

NSA is the Q1 outlier (positive) — and ironically, the most likely to be irrelevant by year-end. NSA delivered +2.0% same-store NOI growth, +0.9% revenue growth (from −0.7% in Q4), and −3.9% expense growth — the best operating quarter in the sector. The drivers: (1) lapping the company's PRO consolidation costs from 2024, which dropped personnel / utilities / R&M; (2) a +37% marketing spend in Q4'25 driving 11% YoY move-in volume gains that carried into Q1; (3) Atlanta and Phoenix improving sequentially. NSA's relevance to ASSI is now mostly as a benchmark — once PSA closes the acquisition in Q3, NSA stops reporting standalone results.

So what for ASSI The convergence on revenue says ASSI should track sector revenue trends; the divergence on expenses says NC operators should benchmark themselves to NSA/PSA cost discipline, not CUBE/EXR — Triangle-area expenses don't have the weather randomness of urban Mid-Atlantic / Northeast operators.

06 NC Operator Intelligence Brief

Where ASSI's submarkets sit relative to the sector tape

This section is the operational read for ASSI: where ASSI's submarkets sit relative to the sector tape and what the REITs are doing that a 20-facility NC operator should track.

6A. NC / Southeast market signals

REIT commentary on Sun Belt and Southeast in Q1'26 was directional and a little contradictory.

Our more stable urban markets in the Northeast and Midwest continue to outperform, while our more transient supply-impacted markets across the Sun Belt and the West Coast are beginning to see green shoots in the form of second-derivative improvement. — Christopher P. Marr, President & CEO, CubeSmart (Q1 2026 earnings call)

EXR called out Atlanta, Austin, Dallas, Miami, and Phoenix as continuing supply pressure — Charlotte was not named in either the EXR or PSA market-watch lists. Raleigh-Cary continues to escape direct REIT callouts as a problem market, which has been a consistent pattern since 2023. PSA Q1'26: "Sun Belt supply pressures persisted" — coastal and Midwest outperformed Sun Belt. NSA Q1'26: Atlanta and Phoenix as weakest revenue markets.

No REIT specifically named the Triangle, Charlotte, or Greensboro / High Point as either underperformers or outperformers. This is itself a signal — the Carolinas are flying under the REIT problem-radar, which is constructive for ASSI's narrative to ownership.

6B. Supply pipeline watch

National Yardi Matrix data (April–May 2026):

  • 46.5M NRSF under construction = 2.3% of national inventory, flat for several months
  • May 20, 2026 Yardi update: 2026 supply forecast revised up (~6% from prior estimate) due to stronger-than-expected Q4'25 construction starts
  • Q1'26 construction starts down 29% YoY — meaningful deceleration
  • EXR: % of same-store properties facing new supply down to 8% in 2025, projected 6% in 2026 (vs. high-20s% in 2021–23)

NC-specific (2025 deliveries, StorageCafe data):

  • Charlotte–Concord–Gastonia: 776,800 sf delivered, 3.5% of inventory (above national average)
  • Raleigh–Cary: 385,800 sf delivered, 3.3% of inventory (above national average)
  • Elizabeth City: 265,900 sf, 48.6% of inventory — the #1 metro in the U.S. for rate of supply growth (extreme outlier; not directly comparable but useful as a state-level signal)

A Yardi sub-MSA pull on Greensboro–High Point is the lowest-cost way to validate or relax this flag — recommend running that against ASSI's facility-level rate data for the next ownership check-in.

6C. Demand drivers

Housing turnover remains the structural drag on storage demand. PSA, EXR, and Yardi all explicitly cited "historically low home sales" as a constraint on storage demand in Q1'26. The 10-year Treasury hovered near 4.5% through Q1'26, mortgage rates remained elevated, and home sales activity remained depressed. Until housing turnover recovers, storage demand recovery is going to come from rate moderation (less aggressive pricing competition) rather than from volume growth.

NC structural advantages (re-verifiable, not memorized):

  • NC consistently ranks top-3 nationally for net domestic migration
  • Raleigh-Cary unemployment 3.6% (Nov 2025 Yardi data), Durham 3.8% — both below NC (3.9%) and U.S. (4.5%)
  • 19,600 net jobs added in Raleigh-Cary MSA in 12 months ending Sept 2025
  • Major capital investments (Tower 5 at North Hills, RUS Bus, Dix Park) and ongoing job-growth corridor activity

Forward-looking leading indicator to watch: Existing home sales in Wake, Johnston, Cabarrus counties. When transaction volumes start recovering (likely a Fed-cut-driven 2H 2026 story if it happens), storage demand should follow with a 60–90-day lag.

6E. Rate & pricing intelligence

The Q1'26 pricing environment for an NC regional operator: competition for move-ins remains intense, but floor has been found and rate-side momentum is building. EXR delivered +2.5%/sf new-customer rate growth in Q1; CUBE +2.0% in March and April; SMA +2% on per-unit basis. PSA still negative (−2.4%) but improving. NSA RevPAR +0.6%.

For ASSI, the pricing posture going into rental season:

  • Web / street rates: Should be sticky-to-slightly-up YoY. The REIT consensus is that the rate-cutting era is over — discount activity is moderating.
  • Achieved move-in rates: Where ASSI has flexibility, hold rates and let discount pressure come through promotions rather than published rate cuts.
  • ECRI: Match institutional cadence. The retention data EXR is publishing (64% >12 months) suggests rate elasticity at the existing-customer level is favorable.
  • Concession philosophy: SmartStop's comment that achieved rates per SF declined 7% in Q1 while unit-based rates grew 2% is the clearest tell — the discount layer (free first month, etc.) is doing the work, not headline rate cuts. ASSI's published rates can hold; the concession line is where competitive pressure shows up.
